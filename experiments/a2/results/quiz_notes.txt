QUIZ NOTES — CONCEPTS AND COMPLETED RESULTS

This is a companion to main.tex, not an additional experiment. It includes
all required numerical comparisons. Most LM sweeps use one seed; the stated
paired checks use three initialization seeds with data order held fixed.
Small loss differences do not establish significance or universal rules.

1. What is being scaled?
Problems1–2 vary training-token budget D, not model size: the model stays
the same. Problem3 varies batch B at fixed total examples/tokens, hence
larger B means fewer optimizer updates. Problem4 varies width n or depth L.
Do not interpret a token-budget exponent as a width exponent.

2. Two different fits
Loss versus LR: a local quadratic in x=log2(LR/reference LR),
L=a*x^2+b*x+c. The fitted optimum is reference LR*2^(-b/(2a)).
This is an interpolation choice, not a universal power law for loss.
Three points determine a quadratic exactly; that is not evidence of small
uncertainty. A boundary minimum needs another measurement to bracket it.
Optimal LR versus budget/batch/width: fit log(optimal LR) versus log(scale).
If eta*=eta_ref*(scale/scale_ref)^p, doubling scale multiplies LR by2^p.
p=.5 means four times the batch calls for twice the LR; p=1 means twice
the batch calls for twice the LR. A fitted exponent is conditional on the
optimizer, schedule, budget, data, and sampled range.

3. Problem1: extrapolation is the hard part
The three smallest AdamW budgets suggest LR grows with tokens (p=.2125,
doubling multiplier1.16), but larger supplied budgets reverse that trend.
At4.9152B, the recent-three-budget prediction(.002226) beats the
all-six-budget prediction(.003728) in this one-seed comparison.
Both extrapolate2x beyond their largest source budget. Their source spans
are4x versus32x; including smaller sources does not change that target
extrapolation distance. More data reduces fitting variance but can increase
bias if one power law is a poor description across the entire range.
Hyperball's small-budget fitted LR decreases with tokens, but this alone
does not establish more reliable extrapolation than AdamW.

4. Problem2: LR and weight decay interact
AdamW applies decoupled shrinkage1-eta_t*lambda each update. Eta*lambda
describes instantaneous shrinkage, not the complete optimization trajectory:
changing eta also changes the gradient update. Identical products therefore
do not guarantee identical loss. In the source sweeps, WD's token-budget
power law is tighter than the product's; the product is tighter than LR's.
The product-derived target recipe is practically close to the WD=.1 control,
not demonstrably better (.000416 higher measured loss in one seed).

5. Problem3: noise competes with fewer updates
At fixed training tokens, a larger batch averages out more gradient noise
but gives fewer updates. Larger LR can partly compensate; it cannot create
the missing update opportunities. In the LM source sweep, eta*(B) has
p=.5878, so doubling batch multiplies fitted LR by1.503: near, but not
exactly, square-root scaling. Fixed-LR WD scaling has p=.7012 (factor1.626).
The fixed-LR/scaled-WD target recipes outperform the fixed-WD/scaled-LR
recipes at128 and256 in the measured runs. Both fitted LR and WD increase
with batch, but they were fitted in separate conditional sweeps, not jointly.

6. What the noisy quadratic model (NQM) does and does not predict
It isolates curvature, gradient noise, initialization, and update count.
It is not a miniature language model. SGD/RMSprop/Adam source exponents
need not imply the same accuracy when transferred to a held-out batch.
In the high-noise adaptive-optimizer experiments, exponents approach .5;
near-linear scaling in a lower-noise setup does not contradict that.
Adam's v_i is a running estimate of E[g_i^2], including squared mean
gradient and noise variance; it is not just the gradient variance.
Bias correction compensates for initializing moving averages at zero.
The original NQM has no WD, so it cannot directly validate a WD scaling law.

7. Momentum: compare like with like
For the LM momentum sweep, LR and WD stay fixed within each batch.
Best sampled beta1 is .95 atB8 and .9 atB256. Gains over zero momentum
are .04124 and .17521 loss, respectively. Very high momentum(.99) hurtsB256.
The toy sweep retuned LR at every coefficient; the LM sweep did not.
Do not transfer exact best coefficients from the toy loss to an LM.
Momentum can smooth noise and preserve useful directions, but excessive
memory slows adaptation, especially when a fixed token budget gives few steps.

8. Problem4.0: three constraints, two independent assumptions
The abc layer has z=n^(-a)*W*x, Var(W0)=sigma^2*n^(-2b), LR=eta*n^(-c).
Let alpha describe update/input alignment and omega describe initial
readout/feature-change alignment. Full alignment has exponent1; the
independent random reference has exponent.5. These assumptions are separate.
Hidden initialization: a+b=.5. Direct update: a+c=alpha.
Readout response to feature movement: a+b=omega.
With hidden a=0 and readout c=0, hidden=(0,.5,alpha),
readout=(alpha,omega-alpha,0); fixed-fan-in input=(0,0,0).
LR exponents are c; initialization VARIANCE exponents are2b, not b.
The full/full case gives hidden LR eta/m, readout multiplier1/m,
readout variance1/n0, with m=n/n0. At m=1 the reference recipe is unchanged.
The interaction term also needs the stated alignment bound to stay bounded.

9. Problem4.1: transfer is not the same as best achievable loss
A base LR is the tunable scalar before the per-layer multipliers.
Width muP changes readout initialization, forward scale and hidden LR;
it does not assign eta/m to embeddings or normalization gains.
Depth-muP adds residual r^(-.5), block LR r^(-.5), block epsilon r^(-.5).
CompleteP adds residual r^(-1), unchanged block LR, block epsilon r^(-1).
Depth block rules include within-block norms, not embedding/readout/final norm.
Compare transferred-LR loss and retuned loss separately.
Bounded means not exploding. Nonvanishing means not shrinking to zero.
Unit RMSNorm output can hide a growing pre-normalization residual stream.
Measure branch RMS BEFORE its depth multiplier and movement from initial
features, not just the current feature magnitude.

10. Readout alignment is not a yes/no detector
The ratio omega_move uses the INITIAL readout and feature CHANGE h_t-h_0.
It is undefined before features move. Actual-update alpha uses the matrix
change, its PRE-update input, and that matrix's actual fan-in.
Pooling over a vocabulary much larger than width can constrain omega near
.5 even for readout-dependent directions. A .5 measurement alone is not
proof of independence or absent feature learning; inspect movement too.
The width-law exponent p is fitted across widths. Alpha and omega instead
come from matrix/probe ratios at one width and update; don't confuse them.

11. Problem4.2: longer training adds confounders to interpret
Source-only width predictions must be recorded before held-out target runs.
At n0=512, the baseline and width-muP models exactly coincide.
The longer runs have2344updates and23warmupsteps; the first LR iszero.
Their first-five-update probes are not the same full-rate intervention
as the constant-LR stress test. Shared embedding/readout Gaussian draws
also mean initial features and initial readout are not strictly independent.
RMSNorm divides by sqrt(mean(x^2)+epsilon). If feature scale is comparable
to sqrt(epsilon), epsilon changes signal scale rather than being negligible.
Baseline embedding RMS~1/n approaches that threshold as width grows;
muP embedding RMS~1/512 holds this particular effect approximately fixed.
Clipping, normalization, WD, warmup and decay are held fixed as instructed,
not removed to force theory to fit. Above the reference width, muP's smaller
hidden LR weakens per-step hidden WD shrinkage at fixed lambda; below it,
the larger hidden LR strengthens shrinkage. A shifted best LR alone does
not establish that a model is outside a stable muP regime.
Adam normalizes by running moments: clipping a gradient by a factor does
not generally shrink the parameter update by that same factor. Measure
actual updates rather than inferring them from gradient norms alone.

12. The handout's copied-model example is a different construction
Copying width640 by k replicates features/heads and uses W/k in every
hidden block and V/k in each readout copy, with NO extra forward multiplier.
For negligible Adam epsilon, positive gradient scaling cancels in the
normalized update. To preserve the stored weights'1/k relation, both
hidden and readout LRs must be .001/k; embedding and norm LRs stay .001.
Thus k4 uses .00025/.00025/.001 (option3), and k8 uses
.000125/.000125/.001 (option2). QK norm gains are shared within each
64-dimensional head and remain unscaled. Copies are separate parameters,
not tied weights. Standard muP instead stores an unscaled readout and puts
1/m in the forward pass, so its readout LR stays eta. Do not combine the
copied-model LR rule with the independent-initialization muP recipe blindly.
This is the handout's analytical answer, not an additional measured run.
